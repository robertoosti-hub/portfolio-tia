## Living terraces: why mountain vineyards matter

In the valley where I live, vineyards climb the slopes on narrow terraces held up by dry-stone walls. Some of these walls were built more than a hundred years ago. Today many terraces are abandoned, because working them by hand takes a lot of time and the income is low.

When a terrace is abandoned, the forest comes back quickly. This is not only a loss of landscape: without maintenance, the walls collapse and the slope becomes less stable. For this reason, a vineyard on a terrace is also a way of protecting the territory.

My project, which I call *Living terraces*, aims to recover a small area of abandoned terraces next to my family farm. I would like to plant disease-resistant grape varieties, which need fewer treatments, and to install sensors that measure leaf wetness and temperature. With these data it is possible to decide when a treatment is really necessary.

I also want to tell the story of this work. Visitors are often surprised when they see how steep our vineyards are. A short guided walk “from the wall to the glass” could help people understand why a bottle from these slopes has a different value.

I know that many details still need to be checked: costs, permits and which varieties are allowed. However, I believe that young people can make mountain agriculture sustainable, if we combine traditional knowledge with new tools.
